Finding Travel Opportunities

Many people dream of travelling the world, but it can be hard to find opportunities that align professionally.
Especially as an engineering student, where it seems like you're not the target audience for these opportunities.

I had spent lots of time dreaming about travelling the world early in college. I watched others do it and I wanted to find those opportunities myself. But Fulbright is of course a competitive application, and part of what makes a competitive applicant is whether you can prove your adaptability to foreign environments. Any opportunities that get you out of familiar environments will help with this.

One day during my first semester of four-year school, I had taken a rather unorthodox route to get to class. The door outside the building had faded paint and dents, and it was the kind of door where you didn’t know if it would take you to class or to another dimension.

It led to a stairwell. A rather uninspiring one. Bare, gray concrete. It was chilly inside, and while slowly creeping up the stairs, I came across a bulletin board with a few scattered flyers on it. Some of them had torn pages, some had scribbled handwriting. But one of them was a call for applications. And soon enough, I had myself a summer research internship in Germany.

Two years later, I found a partially-folded sheet of paper, laying on the ground with footprints on it. On that paper was a QR code to RSVP for an info session that led me to a summer internship in South Korea.

I personally see this as ridiculously lucky, as very few pieces of paper you find laying around campus will have such a grand opportunity. But the truth is, I have seen many programs that send engineering students out to see the real world, in places you would least expect. Some of these opportunities aren’t even found in the engineering buildings; you might have to go into places you’ve never explored to find such opportunities.

You have probably heard of the analogy where you do not know how many red cars you saw today, but you could count easily if you started paying attention. Or the moment you learn about something cool, you start to see it pop up everywhere.

1: Keep your eyes open. Constantly be on the lookout for opportunities.

My summer internship in Germany was transformative for me. It was one where to this day, I still tell stories about it. Now imagine how much I talked about it when I came back for the fall semester. It was the only thing on my mind. You might find my physical presence, but mentally I was off exploring every nook and cranny of our world. I started building an obsession for street view on Google Maps, because that’s the closest I could get to exploring.

Maybe I became annoying to some people. But I shared my experiences with another faculty member, and they referred me to a two-week study abroad program in Mexico to take a course on supply chain management. Yet another opportunity for me to leave my comfort zone and explore a new culture.

So maybe to my friends and family, I was annoying. But by sharing what inspired me and what I really wanted to do to professors and academic advisors, they helped connect me with opportunities I wouldn’t know exist otherwise.

2: Let faculty and academic advisors know your goals. Tell them exactly what you want to do. Tell them your wildest dreams about travelling. And be really, really passionate about it.

But to build these exciting experiences for yourself, you don’t even have to be somewhere abroad. Literally anything that can take you out of town is a great opportunity.

I was fortunate enough to spend most of my undergrad experience working on a team that built weather balloon payloads. It ended up being my senior project. Now depending on who you talk to, this may not be as glamorous as building the next reusable rocket, or building an autonomous RC aircraft that flies perfectly between cones in a field. But it gave me opportunities to travel. I got to explore northern Nevada with my colleagues, going off-roading while tracking down the weather balloon we just launched. We travelled to Arkansas to launch our weather balloon during the total solar eclipse (which is a story that I’ve told to people way too much at this point).

During my graduate studies, I joined an outing club that would organize hiking and other outdoor trips. I was also a part of an astronomy club during my undergrad, where we travelled some distances to get away from the light pollution. I was looking for literally anything to get me travelling somewhere. This isn’t always going to be on your university’s rocket club or FSAE team, so you have to get creative with it. This sort of circles back to the first tip about keeping your eyes open, but I mean keeping your eyes open for anything.

3: Find field research opportunities. Find clubs that go out and explore. Find a group of friends who also enjoy exploration. Seek out and surround yourself with those who go out in the field for their work. Soon enough, that will be you.

And finally, something that can really be applied to lots of things in life. Some of my most formative experiences were ones where I simply traveled to a conference in another state. I met so many fascinating people during those times, and even going to a new state I had never been to was an incredible amount of fun. It didn’t even matter where I went; what mattered more is that I always felt good after trying something new.

4: Be the first to raise your hand to go. Take every exploration opportunity you can. No matter how big or small. Every opportunity is a good one.
